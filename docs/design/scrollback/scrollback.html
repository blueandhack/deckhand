Scrollback

Session history stops being a paged list of previews and becomes one continuous transcript — newest at the bottom, drag up for older. Deleting the pager's chrome returns seven lines of text to the screen.

Now, and proposed

Both panels are drawn at 1× real geometry — 320×480, Spleen 8×16 on an 8px advance, the live DARK palette straight out of THEMES[]. The right-hand panel is really scrollable: drag it.

NOW — paged previews
One screen at a time, 300-character previews, a round trip per page. A scrubber and three buttons own the bottom 120px. Tapping a row opens a second, separate pager to read one message whole.
PROPOSED — one transcript
Full message text inline, ASCII gutter markers per role, drag to scroll. No scrubber, no buttons, no second screen — and 25 lines of text where there were 18.

The gutter is the whole design

Claude Code marks each turn in a gutter rather than in a bubble, which is the right instinct for a 320px-wide panel: bubbles spend horizontal space, and horizontal space is the scarce axis here. But Claude Code's own markers cannot be drawn on this device. Spleen declares 0x20..0x7E, so ⏷ and ├ render as nothing at all — the trap this repo has already paid for five times, most recently with a truncation marker that was invisible for months. So the vocabulary is ASCII, one column wide, and colour is never the only carrier.

MarkRoleColour tokenTreatment
>You COLOR_ACCENTFull text, wrapped to 34 columns. The turn that started everything.
*Claude COLOR_GOODFull text, wrapped. The bulk of the transcript.
$Ran a tool COLOR_LABELOne line, dim, clipped with three ASCII dots. Tool name plus its one interesting field.
|Result COLOR_LABELOne line, dim, tucked against the call above it with no spacer — they read as one unit.
!Denied / error COLOR_BADOne line. The only entry that keeps full colour weight while dim.

Why 34 columns

The lane is 12 + 16 (gutter) → 28, then 34 × 8px of text ending at 299, a 6px gap, a 4px scrollbar at 306, and a 10px right margin. Sums to exactly 320.

The pager gets 37 columns today, so the gutter costs three columns, 8%. That is what buys a transcript you can read as a conversation instead of a list of labelled rows.

Why the header keeps a back key

Removing the buttons removes CLOSE, so the header takes a < key at 46×46 — exactly TAP_MIN, the 7.1mm fingertip floor.

The name lane between it and the filter chip is 178px = 22 columns, which is precisely the 22 characters the host already caps a session name to. Derived, not chosen.

Why a scrollbar at all

The counter says 412/628, but a number cannot track a finger. A 4px rail is the only thing that answers “how much is above me” continuously while you drag, and it replaces a 46px scrubber band.

It is drawn only when the transcript is taller than the view, so a three-message session shows none.

What this deletes

The strongest argument for the design is subtraction. Scrolling makes four separate mechanisms unnecessary, and one of them is an entire screen with its own touch handler and wire verb.

  • The second-level pager. drawHistFull(), handleHistFullTouch() and the HISTORY <id> <filter> item:<n> verb with its host handler sendHistoryItem() — all gone, because full text is already inline. This existed only because a message taller than one screen was otherwise unreachable.
  • The scrubber. 46px of tap band plus a 20px track, replaced by a 4px rail that does the same job continuously rather than by jump.
  • PREV / CLOSE / NEXT. A 50px button row plus its margins. CLOSE survives as the header's back key; the other two are what scrolling is.
  • Per-screen round trips. histPaginate() and the page/pages/from bookkeeping stop being per-turn work — the device fetches once and then owns the whole transcript.

Set against that: the gutter costs three columns, and a very long message costs real scrolling — a 4,000-character turn is about 118 lines, roughly five screens.

Engineering notes

Fetch once, into PSRAM

Board 1's reader holds one screen in a 2.4KB arena because that board has ~26KB of free heap. Board 2 has 8,388,608 bytes of PSRAM, of which the framebuffer takes 307,200. A real transcript's whole conversation is 122KB; everything including tool calls is 584KB.

So the constraint that shaped the paged design does not exist here, and the fetch can be the whole thing. Over board 2's native USB CDC — measured at ~384 KB/s from the SCREENSHOT path — that is ~0.3s.

BLE cannot have it

The same 122KB over BLE at ~666 B/s is 183 seconds. So the request carries a byte budget and the host returns the newest entries that fit, plus a count of what it withheld.

The top of the scroll then states the limit rather than hiding it — the same rule that makes POWERPROBE refuse with not on battery instead of going quiet. Never offer a control that cannot work.

The flush is the frame budget

A full-screen flush is 30ms measured (gather 8.3 + transfer 21.8). The list area is 412 of 480 rows, so a scrolled frame is ~23ms derived by proportion — derived, not measured, and worth measuring with PERF before anyone trusts it.

That puts the ceiling near 40fps and says the bottleneck is QSPI, not glyphs. The shim owns the framebuffer, so a scroll should memmove rows and draw only the newly exposed band.

The one open engineering question

Pixel-smooth or quantised to the 16px cell? Toggle it on the panel above and drag. Quantised means the memmove is always a whole number of lines and no glyph is ever drawn clipped — which suits a bitmap font on an exact 16px cell, and suits this repo's preference for exact arithmetic. But 16px is 2.5mm at 6.49 px/mm, and a slow drag in 2.5mm steps is visibly notchy.

Smooth scrolling needs the shim to clip a partial glyph row, which I have not verified it can do — that is the first thing to establish, because it decides this. If it cannot, quantised is not a preference but the only option.

Not decided, and not mine to decide

  • Live tailing. A working session keeps producing entries. “Newest at the bottom” invites the transcript to grow while you watch it, with a 2 NEW affordance when you have scrolled away — but that is a second feature with its own refresh cadence, and it is not in this mock. Snapshot-on-open is what is drawn.
  • Momentum. Every fling frame is another ~23ms flush, so a long decay is expensive in a way it would not be on a phone. Drawn here as a short decay; worth feeling on real glass before fixing.
  • Board 1 keeps its pager. Everything here sits behind a board flag. Board 1 is resistive, where this repo already measured that drag-scroll misfires, and its binary is held byte-identical.

What this mock is not evidence of. Nothing here has been on the glass. It is a browser rendering at the device's arithmetic, so it vouches for geometry and for the interaction — and for nothing else. Colour in particular it cannot settle: on board 2 even SCREENSHOT reads the shadow framebuffer rather than the panel, so COLORTEST is the instrument and a person is the authority.
